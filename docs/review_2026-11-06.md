### New findings (not in the earlier docs)

**N1. The SA formula in the brief drops H&P's inflation adjustment.**
Hadlock & Pierce (2010, fn. 2, p. 1912; §3.2, p. 1929) define Size as the log of *inflation-adjusted (to 2004)* book assets, Age as years on Compustat with a stock price, and cap Size at $4.5bn and Age at 37 years. The brief (§2.2) uses nominal US$ at each year's average rate, founding-year Age and 1/99 winsorizing. The project follows the brief. Two of the three differences were already in open question #7; the inflation adjustment was not.
Effect on the main model: a deflator is a year-specific constant in ln(assets), so in the linear term it is absorbed by year FE; it only enters through the Size² term. The size of that effect cannot be computed without a deflator series, which is not in `raw_data/` and must not be typed from memory. Action: disclose in Methodology; supply a CPI series with its source only if the professor wants H&P's exact definition.
Related check (exploratory, model (h) in Part C): SA rebuilt with H&P's caps in nominal dollars gives β = 4.13 (SE 9.18, p = 0.658), so the null in Table 4 does not depend on the 1/99 rule.

**N2. The Hausman test does not reject because FE is imprecise, not because FE and RE agree.**
FE β(SA) = +2.49 (non-robust SE 6.65); RE β(SA) = -4.88 (non-robust SE 2.15). The difference is 7.37, but its SE is large, so H = 3.10. With firm-clustered SE the RE coefficient is -4.88 (SE 2.29, p = 0.045 with t(21)).
So if the brief's §3.2 rule were applied literally ("p ≥ 0.05: random effects is acceptable"), the headline result would change from "no effect" to "negative and borderline significant" (direction of H1). This should be stated openly in the report, and open question #20 now carries these numbers. Recommendation unchanged: keep the two-way FE of §3.3 as the main model, because FE is consistent under both hypotheses and §3.3 asks for it, and report the RE number as context.

**N3. The cross-sectional SA–TESG association is a firm-size association.**
Pooled OLS with year FE: β(SA) = -5.52 (CR1 p = 0.016 with t(23); wild cluster bootstrap p = 0.095). Adding SIZE: β(SA) = -1.99 (p = 0.334), SIZE = 3.76 (p < 0.001). Replacing SA by its parts: Size 4.16 (p < 0.001), Age 0.06 (p = 0.494). Larger firms have higher TESG; once size is held fixed, SA adds nothing.

**N4. The §6.2 template ("a one-unit increase in the SA Index") would mislead here.**
After firm and year FE, the sd of SA is 0.042 (0.32% of its variance), so one unit of SA is about 24 within-firm standard deviations. Report effects per within-firm sd (Part C) and keep the template's SIZE sentence out of the main-model paragraph (open question #2).

**N5. Environment note.** `.venv/` was built on macOS and does not run on Linux. Reproducibility is confirmed with the versions listed in Part A, which differ slightly from `requirements-lock.txt` (pandas 3.1.1 there) without changing any number.

Reading: within firms, changes in financial constraints as measured by SA are not associated with changes in TESG. The estimate is small and the confidence interval is centred near zero. **Neither H1 nor H2 is supported.** The interval rules out within-firm effects larger than roughly ±1.3 TESG points per typical within-firm change in SA, but it cannot rule out smaller effects.

### C3. Why the result is a null (exploratory, `scripts/06_exploratory_interpretation.py`)

| Model (not the brief's design unless stated) | β on SA | SE | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Two-way FE, brief main | 2.49 | 14.32 | 0.864 |
| (b) RE + year dummies, clustered | -4.88 | 2.29 | 0.045 |
| (c) Pooled OLS + year FE, clustered | -5.52 | 2.13 | 0.016 (wild 0.095) |
| (d) as (c) + SIZE | -1.99 | 2.02 | 0.334 |
| (f) Hybrid RE: deviation from firm mean | 1.80 | 13.36 | 0.894 |
| (f) Hybrid RE: firm mean | -5.05 | 2.50 | 0.056 |
| (g) Two-way FE without 1216 | 5.11 | 14.99 | 0.737 |
| (h) Two-way FE, SA with nominal H&P caps | 4.13 | 9.18 | 0.658 |

Three points follow.
1. **Almost all SA variation is between firms.** Between-firm sd 0.80 vs within-firm 0.042. The brief's firm FE removes the variation in which SA is informative. Hadlock & Pierce (2010, §3.4, p. 1931) say the index "will be more effective in detecting the cross-sectional variation in constraints than the time variation".
2. **Between firms, more constrained firms have lower TESG (direction of H1), but this is size.** One between-firm sd of SA corresponds to about -3.9 TESG points in RE, and the association disappears once SIZE is controlled (d, e). With SA built only from size and age, the data cannot separate "constrained" from "small".
3. **The null is not driven by one firm or by the winsorizing rule** (g, h).

### C4. Fit with the literature in `literature/`

- Chang et al. (2025, p. 5 and Table 2) also find an insignificant coefficient for the SA index in Taiwan (2015 to 2021, TEJ ESG), unlike KZ and WW, and attribute it to the index's sensitivity to firm size. Their model runs in the opposite direction (FC on lagged ESG) with year and industry FE, so it is a parallel, not a replication.
- Hadlock & Pierce (2010, §3.4, p. 1931) recommend year effects and warn that SA captures cross-sectional more than time variation, which matches C3.

### C5. Draft result paragraph (replaces the §6.2 template; numbers from the scripts)

> Table 4 reports the two-way fixed-effects estimates for TWSE-listed food firms over 2016–2025 (N = 219 firm-year observations, 24 firms, 22 with at least two observations). The coefficient on the SA Index is 2.489 (CR1 standard error 14.316; t = 0.17), with p = 0.864 using t(21) critical values and p = 0.880 from a wild cluster bootstrap. Because firm and year fixed effects leave little variation in SA (within-firm standard deviation 0.042), we scale the estimate: a one-standard-deviation within-firm increase in SA, i.e. tighter constraints, is associated with a 0.10-point change in the TESG score (95% CI -1.14 to 1.35). The evidence therefore supports neither H1 nor H2. The result holds with the median-split dummy, the lagged SA Index and with SIZE as an additional control (Table 5). Among the controls, none is significant at the 10% level in the main model; leverage is negative (-9.40) and significant at 10% only in the lagged-SA specification.

### C6. Limitations to state

1. SA is identified from within-firm changes in US$ assets after FE (N4, D1); part of that change is the NTD/USD rate, common to all firms each year and mostly absorbed by year FE.
2. 22 effective clusters: inference uses t(G-1) and wild bootstrap (Cameron & Miller 2015).
3. TESG for data years 2016 to 2020 is retrospective (announced 2022-03-31, D2).
4. Food firms have had mandatory CSR reporting since 2014 (CLAUDE.md §9, Tseng 2022; page to be confirmed before citing), which may limit within-firm ESG variation.
5. SA definition differs from H&P in three ways (N1).
